I

The Punishment

Sisyphus was the king of Corinth and one of the cleverest and most cunning humans. As punishment from the gods for his deceit, he was forced to roll a massive boulder up a steep hill, but just before it reached the top, it would always slip from him, and he would have to start over, over and over, forever.

II

The Offense

This maddening punishment was the consequence of his arrogant belief that his intelligence could outsmart Zeus. He had made a bold move by exposing Zeus’s scandals and romantic escapades, and he told the river god Asopus all that had happened to his daughter Aegina, whom Zeus had kidnapped. Regardless of Zeus’s wrongdoing, Sisyphus clearly overstepped his bounds, seeing himself as an equal to the gods entitled to expose their foolishness and recklessness.

III

Death in Chains

So Zeus sent Death, that is Thanatos, to seize his soul. But Sisyphus tricked him by chaining and imprisoning him, stopping death on Earth, and no one died. The balance of the universe was disturbed until the gods intervened, freed Thanatos, and Sisyphus’s soul was taken to the underworld.

IV

The Last Trick

Yet Sisyphus didn’t give up. Before his death, he instructed his wife not to perform the usual burial rites for him, then complained to Hades, the god of the underworld, about her negligence, and asked for permission to return to Earth in order to admonish her. Hades believed him and granted permission, but Sisyphus never went back to the underworld afterward; he lived for many long years on Earth, laughing at the very god of death.

V

Eternal Punishment

As a result, Zeus showed his own cleverness: he sent Sisyphus back to the underworld and bound him to an eternal punishment and perplexity. That is why endless, purposeless tasks are called Sisyphean.

VI

The Legacy

Sisyphus and his story were a popular subject for ancient writers, and the painter Polygnotus depicted him on the walls of the Lesche at Delphi.

A perspective

The Other Side of the Stone

One must imagine Sisyphus happy.Albert Camus, The Myth of Sisyphus

Sisyphus tricked the gods and revealed Zeus’s secrets, so his punishment was to roll a boulder to the top of a steep hill, only for it to slip from his hands and crash down just as he was about to reach the top, forcing him to start over. They wanted his torment to never end.

But we read the story from another perspective. We see that Sisyphus wasn’t searching for the summit; he was looking for a way to endure the journey. Reaching the top might be delayed, or it might never happen, but a heart that finds joy in each step can keep going. Here, happiness isn’t a reward that comes at the end; it’s the sustenance that carries the traveler along the way.

We believe everyone has their own boulder and hill, and those who learn to smile on the climb find their burden lighter. So don’t postpone your joy until you arrive—take it with you. In it lies the strength to reach the end.

The gods wanted to break him with repetition, so he turned repetition into a lovely habit, fatigue into warmth, and the boulder into a traveling companion. And we strive to make Sisyphus happy, because one who finds joy on the journey can truly go the distance.
